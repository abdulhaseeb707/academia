Inflation and Corporate Profitability: An Econometric Analysis

An empirical investigation into macro-financial transmission mechanisms across firm-level operating performance.

1. Executive Summary & Research Motivation

While macroeconomic theory frequently asserts that inflation exerts uniform cost-push pressures on corporate margins, real-world corporate financial statements demonstrate significant heterogeneity. This project treats the inflation-profitability nexus as an empirical question, investigating how firm size, capital structure, and sector-specific pricing power mediate inflationary impacts on corporate return metrics (ROA, ROE, EBITDA margins).

2. Econometric Formulation & Hypothesis

The baseline empirical model evaluates corporate profitability as a function of macroeconomic indicators and firm-level control variables:

Profitability_{i,t} = β₀ + β₁·Inflation_{t} + β₂·PolicyRate_{t} + β₃·FirmSize_{i,t} + β₄·Leverage_{i,t} + μ_{i} + ε_{i,t}
  • Primary Hypothesis: High inflationary episodes exert a statistically significant contractionary effect on operating margins for high-leverage and non-consumer staple firms.
  • Secondary Hypothesis: Capital-intensive corporations with high asset tangibility possess asymmetric transmission lags compared to services and financial intermediaries.

3. Data Architecture & Empirical Pipeline

The empirical workflow is developed in dual environments—Stata for standard econometrics diagnostics and Python for automated data cleaning, pipeline scripting, and visualization:

  • Data Cleaning & Harmonization: Normalization of balance sheet items, handling missing observations, and adjusting nominal figures.
  • Panel Specification Testing: Running Hausman specification tests to determine appropriateness between Fixed Effects (Within Estimator) and Random Effects (GLS).
  • Diagnostic Suite: Testing for heteroskedasticity (Breusch-Pagan/Cook-Weisberg), autocorrelation (Wooldridge test for AR(1)), and multicollinearity (Variance Inflation Factors - VIF).
  • Robust Standard Errors: Applying Huber-White cluster-robust standard errors to correct for cross-sectional dependence.

4. Key Insights & Findings

The findings highlight that inflation's impact on corporate profitability is heavily dependent on a firm's pricing power and debt structure. Highly leveraged firms experience compounded profitability decline as monetary tightening often accompanies inflation, raising financial expenses while input costs increase.
